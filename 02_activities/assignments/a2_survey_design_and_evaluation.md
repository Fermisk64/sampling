## Part B - Survey Evaluation:

Identify and describe survey features:

```
1. Sample type: Stratified design employing probability sampling. It is cross-sectional and one individual 15+ of age is randomly selected from sampled housholds. 


2. Sample size: A field sample of about 50,000 units was used. Among them, about 40,000 invitation letters were sent out to the selected households to complete the electronic questionnaire. A completion of 24,000 questionnaires was expected.

3. Target population: Individuals 15+ years living in private housholds in Canada's ten provinces.  It excludes full-time (residing for more than six months) residents of institutions. 

4. Sampling frame: It is from a combined list of landline and cell phone numbers that Statistics Canada links to home addresses. Each record represents a household, which may have one phone number or several. If a number can’t be matched to an address, it’s still kept on its own. Using both landline and mobile numbers helps ensure the survey reaches as many types of households as possible.
Rejective sampling was used to account for those out of reach.

5. Survey mode(s): Electronic questionaire and computer assissted telephone interviewing (CATI)

6. Timeline: one time survey, cross-sectional, covering from 2017-2018 (2018 cycle only). The responses were collected from September to December 2018.

7. Response rate: The overall response rate was 41.9%

8. Weights
WGHT_PER: at the individual/person level
Bootstrap weights: for design based variance estimation

9. Data processing:
    - SSPE processing tool to allow tha staff to process data in a timely and structured manner
    - using a structured environment to monitor the processing of the data and ensure best practices and harmonized business processes were followed
    - automated and manual edits (family, consistency, and flow edits) at various macro and micro levels of data processing stages, to check and ensure the integrity of the matrix data
    - Using CATI allow a valid range of codes for each question and built-in edits, and automatically follows the flow of the questionnaire. Once any out of range value was identified, they were immediately resolved with the respondent
    - similar checks to CATI were performed by the head office

10. Cleaning, imputation, etc: Weight adjustments and 
Estimations: 
    Each person in the sample represents many others in the population, and this “representation” is captured through their survey weight. For this survey, weights were also adjusted to account for the subsampling of non‑volunteers and then further aligned so the income distribution matched reliable 2017 provincial income data. These adjustments help ensure the final estimates accurately reflect the Canadian population.
    The survey weights are further adjusted so the results match known population totals, like monthly age‑sex estimates for each province. Aligning the data with these external benchmarks makes the final estimates more accurate and precise
 Imputations : 
    Imputation was mainly done using donor records, the system looked for respondents with similar characteristics and used their answers to fill in missing values. If no suitable donor could be found, a mean value from similar respondents was used instead. Income, volunteering, donation, and solicitation variables were imputed in a structured nine‑step process. Most income data came from tax record linkage, and only the remaining missing cases were imputed. Overall, the process filled in incomplete answers using information from similar respondents, increasing the number of usable cases for analysis.
For quality assurance:
    Statisticians carefully checked the data before release by comparing results over time, running cross‑tabulations, and checking against other data sources. 
Data disclosure: 
    To protect confidentiality, Statistics Canada suppresses or modifies any information that could identify a respondent. 

11. Sources of error
    - Sampling error: does not cover the entire sampling frame and also vriance inherent in the sampling
    - Non-sampling error: Due to imperfect coverage and non-response. Happening at both household and individual level
    - Coverage error: excludes households that have no means to participate. This also causes bias.
    - Subsampling error: introduced by the "rejective sampling", and dividing the non-volunteers into groups of short and long interviews.
    - measurement error: due to over- or under- state responses
 

12. Limitations, known biases, etc: 
    
    - The geographic coverage includes only the 10 provinces and excludes territories and institutional organizations. Therefore, the results canot be generalized beyond the survey populations.
    - It is cross sectional and covers only from 2017-2018, and does not follow individuals over time. Thus the results are limited and cannot be references for other periods.
    - It is based on self-report and responses from individuals, thus the reponses can be over- or under- reported. Similarly, the volunteers could have over-stated their participations.
    - Selection bias: it excluded territotries and also the full-time residents of insitutions
```
